Dotted drops: the pressure on the piston falls the instant a weight comes off. Solid runs: the gas expands against that constant pressure. Dashed curve: where the gas would go if the same mass came off as many tiny weights.

Every setting starts at 2.00 atm and ends at 1.00 atm. Fewer, heavier weights mean the gas does less work as it expands, so it ends up warmer. With infinitely many tiny weights the temperature falls all the way to the dashed mark on the thermometer.

Lift the weights off or put them back. The reservoir underneath keeps the gas at 300 K the whole time.

Dotted jumps: the pressure on the piston changes the instant a weight comes off or goes on. Solid runs: the gas expands or is squeezed against that constant pressure. Dashed curve: the path for many tiny weights, where PV stays constant because T does.

Because T never changes, the internal energy of the gas never changes, so the heat that flows in or out always equals the work the gas does. Lifting the weights off and putting them all back returns the gas to its starting state, but the work and heat readouts do not return to zero: pushing the piston back down takes more work than the gas gave up on the way out, and the difference leaves as heat.
